The Tundra exception — the mistake we see most

2013–2021 Tundras do not take regular gear lube in the transfer case. Toyota specs its own Genuine 75W transfer gear oil for these trucks — a distinctly lighter fluid than traditional 75W-90 — and our own AMSOIL fitment guide deliberately lists no substitute for those years. Pouring in a generic "75W-90 for everything" is the most common transfer-case mistake discussed on owner forums, and it's the wrong call here: use the Toyota fluid. (The differentials on these same trucks do take standard gear oil — that's exactly why the mix-up happens.)

And the 2022+ Tundra changed the rules again: its transfer case is ATF-filled (about 2.2 quarts) — gear oil doesn't belong in it at all.

OEM vs aftermarket synthetic — the honest answer

  • Where the case specs standard 75W-85 or 80W-90 gear oil (most of the table above): a quality 100% synthetic GL-5 like AMSOIL Severe Gear meets the spec with stronger high-temperature film protection — a genuine upgrade for towing, larger tires, off-road, and winter duty.
  • Where Toyota specs a special fluid (2013–2021 Tundra 75W; 2022+ Tundra ATF): stay OEM or spec-exact. The special spec exists for a reason, and "thicker gear lube" is not an upgrade there — it's the wrong fluid.
  • Transfer case vs differentials: on many Toyotas they share the same 75W-85 spec, so one order covers all three — but verify per vehicle before assuming. The Tundra is the proof.

How often should you change it?

Toyota's factory schedule mostly says "inspect" under severe service — which leaves owners guessing. Our recommendation from the trucks we work on: change transfer case fluid every 30,000 miles if you tow, run larger tires, see off-road or Midwest-winter duty, or run a tuned or supercharged truck. It's one to two quarts of cheap insurance for a case that gets no other protection.
